Appendix C: Universal Pair-Level Failures and Where Reasoning Breaks

Two questions organize this appendix. First, are there TF–gene pairs that every system fails? Second, when a pair fails for every system, where in the mechanistic reasoning does it fail? We answer both by exhaustive enumeration: every one of the 4,632 Task 2 judgments (193 units × 6 systems × 4 runs) and 480 Task 3 judgments (20 units × 6 systems × 4 runs) was inspected, with all six systems holding full four-run adjudication coverage on both tasks. Complete per-unit tables are released as machine-readable supplements, so the enumeration is verifiable rather than illustrative.

C.1 Five pairs fail for every system

On Task 2, exactly five pairs fail the entire five-axis chain in every adjudicated run of every system. They belong to two biological families (Table C1). On the causal-order axis specifically, one further pair — ESR1 → G0S2 — is the benchmark's only perfect universal failure: 24 causal-order failures in 24 unit-runs. On Task 3, no pair is a literal zero, but the three PPARA pairs are rejected in 45 of 48 unit-runs and SMAD3 → PTPRK in 93 of 96 (Table C3).

Beyond these five, the failure landscape falls off smoothly: 16 Task 2 units fail the full chain in all runs of at least four systems (adding the GATA1–TAL1, SRCAP/H2A.Z, and remaining TP63–FOXK1 units), and 37 units fail the chain in at least one run of every system. Two metrics are kept distinct throughout: on the causal-order axis, exactly one unit (T2U_0014) fails in all 24 runs; the three PPARA units each record one axis pass (GPT-6-Astra, run 2 — the same run that resolves the TP63–FOXK1 family), for family axis rates of 95.8–97.2%. On the full chain, the five units of Table C1 fail in all 24 runs, and the ESR1 family's chain failure rate is 98.6% (71/72; its causal-order-axis rate is 97.2%, 70/72 — the single axis pass is Biomni, one run).

C.2 The two families break at different steps

Although the five pairs of Table C1 share the same total outcome — no system ever assembles a correct chain — the per-axis decomposition shows that they break the chain at different steps (Table C2).

The ESR1 pairs break at the first step. No system recognizes the mechanism as chromatin-mediated, and no system names the epigenetic mediator — the dCas9-KRAB/SIN3A-HDAC machinery responsible for the H3K27ac loss appears in none of the 48 traces. Because the narrative starts from "ESR1 binds its element", the error propagates: causal order fails and even the direction logic is dragged down (outcome-link failure 54–58%).

The PPARA pairs break at the partner. Here the direction logic is essentially intact (outcome-link failure 4%): every system correctly predicts that activating PPARA raises the target. What fails is everything between the perturbation and the outcome. BHLHE40 appears in only 3 of the 72 traces (GPT-6-Astra, run 4), which recast it as an opposing factor rather than the required coactivator; the remaining 69 traces answer with a one-layer "PPARA binds its element" narrative. Direction survives; mechanism does not. The benchmark's BHLHE40-depletion units (T2U_0039–0041), whose input names the partner explicitly, sharpen the point: the same six systems pass the full chain there in 23 of 72 unit-runs — failure tracks which component is perturbed and what information the query supplies.

This contrast is the clearest argument for multi-axis adjudication: a single chain-pass bit records both profiles as identical total failures, while the axis decomposition shows that one is a failure of recognition (the experiment is not about what the systems think it is about) and the other a failure of completeness (the systems know the players but omit one).

C.3 In reverse, the same PPARA pairs fail for a different reason

On Task 3 — inferring the intervention from the observed direction — no pair is a literal zero, but the same three PPARA pairs are rejected in 45 of 48 unit-runs each, and SMAD3 → PTPRK in 93 of 96 (Table C3). The answer distributions identify the difficulty: it is not the direction but the experimental design.

For the PPARA pairs the dominant answer is "overexpression" (63 of 48×2 — a gain-of-function guess): systems recover the sign but cannot recover the experimental methodology, which is recoverable only from the source paper. For SMAD3 → PTPRK the difficulty is precision: the experiment co-stimulated NOTCH1 and TGF-β, and systems answer with single-pathway activation. By contrast, the five regulatory-RNA pairs are largely solved (type acceptance 44–45 of 48, ≈94%): inverse inference from an RNA-mediated direction is within reach of every system.

C.4 Summary

Universal pair-level failure is real but rare and structured: five Task 2 pairs, in two families, breaking at two different steps (recognition for ESR1, completeness for PPARA); four near-universal Task 3 pairs, failing on experimental-design inference; and one cross-task pattern — the PPARA pairs fail in both directions, for orthogonal reasons (partner omission forward, unguessable methodology inverse). Universal failure is therefore not noise: it concentrates exactly where mechanistic knowledge, rather than direction statistics, is required.
