MeBench brings mechanism-specific experimental evidence into the evaluation of TF-to-gene reasoning through three complementary tasks built from 171 curated pairs. Its central contribution is to make the dependencies connecting regulatory components to perturbation responses inspectable and testable. Across six systems, the released records reveal correct endpoint predictions accompanied by failed mechanism chains, omitted cooperative dependencies, and named mediators assigned incorrect functional roles. These distinctions give computational biology a concrete basis for evaluating whether a generated explanation can support a mechanism-directed hypothesis.

Future work should expand independently sourced regulatory modules, underrepresented mechanisms and biological contexts under the same evidence requirements. Collaboration with experimental biologists and independent domain experts could support evidence curation, review of compatible interventions, and blinded validation of mechanism scores. Such collaboration would also enable targeted experimental follow-up of disputed dependencies. The released records provide a basis for controlled studies of dependency supervision and context-grounded functional roles, with source- and pair-disjoint evaluation to test generalization. MeBench thus offers a reproducible foundation for broader, collaboratively validated evaluation of biological mechanism reasoning.
