Appendix K — Automated Judge Robustness and Adjudication Sensitivity

Status. Post-freeze sensitivity addendum. No frozen value, judgment file, or main-table number is modified. This appendix re-adjudicates a fixed, seed-disclosed sample of the frozen reasoning chains with a second automated judge and reports how much the five-axis calls — and anything downstream of them — move.

K.1 Design

Sample. Task 2: 360 unit-runs, stratified by system (6 × 60) and by original chain outcome (30 pass / 30 fail per system), with the fail stratum quota-filled to guarantee coverage of each failed axis. Task 3: full enumeration of all 480 unit-runs. Sampling seed 20261017; the full sample list is frozen in sample_design.json. Parsed coverage under the second judge: Task 2 99.4% (358/360), Task 3 100.0%.

Second judge. gemini-3.8-flash — a system that never served as a judge anywhere in the frozen pipeline (the frozen judges were GPT-6-Astra for five systems and Claude-Sonnet-5.5 for GPT-6-Astra). The adjudicator instructions are byte-identical to the shipped rubric, and each sampled row is presented with the same content the original judge saw (input, prediction, reasoning, evaluator-only construction fields). Row keys are unit_id@system@run composites; temperature 0.

Scope caveat (important). This experiment quantifies sensitivity to the choice of automated judge. It cannot establish which judge's calls are biologically correct: the five adjudication axes have no gold standard beyond the evaluator-only construction fields shown to both judges. Agreement here is a property of the pair of judges, not a validation of either. The blinded human adjudication of the frozen judgments (Appendix B.5) remains the open validation step for correctness.

K.2 Axis-wise agreement

Disagreement is strongly structured: the three construct-matching axes (mechanism, mediator, causal order) agree at 92–97% with near-perfect κ on both tasks, while the two interpretive axes (outcome link, no-material-contradiction) carry essentially all of the disagreement.

K.3 Pass-rate difference under the second judge

On the identical sample, the five-axis chain pass rate moves from 50.0% → 52.5% on Task 2 (Δ = 2.5 pp; 11 fail→pass vs 2 pass→fail) and from 60.6% → 64.2% on Task 3 (Δ = 3.5 pp; 33 fail→pass vs 16 pass→fail). Both shifts are small relative to the between-tier system gaps that carry the paper's claims (e.g. GLM-5.3-Flash sits ≈20 pp below the field), but are the same order of magnitude as the spacing inside the mid-field tier — so judge choice adds uncertainty exactly where Appendix M already shows the ordering is not resolved by pair resampling.

K.4 Model ranking stability

A design constraint first. The Task 2 sample is balanced by construction — every system contributes exactly 30 original passes and 30 original fails — so per-system chain-pass levels on this sample are pinned at ≈50% regardless of judge, and the Task 2 sample cannot support any ranking claim. Ranking stability is therefore assessed (i) on the Task 3 full enumeration, where levels are unmanipulated, and (ii) at the population level in Appendix M (pair-level bootstrap).

K.5 Self-preference check

The second judge (gemini-3.8-flash) is itself one of the six evaluated systems (its frozen outputs were judged by GPT-6-Astra, never by itself). If the replacement judge favored its own answers, fail→pass flipping would concentrate on Gemini-origin rows.

Other systems' rows1494 2.7% 16427 16.5%

There is a self-preference signal: on Task 2, 7 of the 11 fail→pass flips (64%) fall on Gemini-origin rows, whose flip rate (23.3%) is far above the other systems' (2.7%); the pattern repeats, attenuated, on Task 3 (24.0% vs 16.5%). Per-axis mean agreement is also slightly higher on Gemini-origin rows (T2 89.0% vs 90.9%; T3 93.5% vs 90.9%). This is direct evidence that automated judges are not exchangeable, and it is the empirical justification for two frozen-pipeline choices: the original design used cross-family judging (a model never judged its own outputs), and chain values remain preliminary pending blinded human adjudication.

K.6 Summary

(i) The construct-matching axes (mechanism, mediator, causal order) are robust to the choice of automated judge: 92–97% agreement, κ 0.77–0.94 on both tasks; disagreement concentrates in the two interpretive axes (outcome link, no-contradiction). (ii) Aggregate chain-pass rates shift by 2.5 pp (T2) and 3.5 pp (T3) under the replacement judge — small against between-tier gaps, but comparable to mid-tier spacing, reinforcing the tier (rather than ranking) reading of the mid-field from Appendix M. (iii) The Task 3 enumeration reproduces the bottom rank and leaves only the top-two adjacency unresolved. (iv) A judge self-preference signal is detectable and is disclosed. (v) Per the scope caveat in K.1, these results establish sensitivity to judge choice, not correctness of any judge; the blinded human adjudication remains the open validation step.
